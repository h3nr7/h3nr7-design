# Voice

The site describes itself as **"a techie journal of tinkering things."** It talks like
Henry: first person, plain, a little dry, never selling. British spelling (colour, artefacts,
neighbouring), en-GB dates ("18 May 2020", "1 October 2026").

## Principles

- **Say the thing.** "That page does not exist. It may have moved, or never have existed."
- **A control names what it does, in the fewest words.** "Back", "Back to home",
  "Print this CV", "Download PDF", "Show more (4)". A link inside a sentence is a word in the
  sentence ("Drop me an **email** for the full CV."); a button is a short imperative.
- **Friendly, not eager.** "Let's have a chat", "Drop me a line", "Otherwise I am always up for
  a coffee and/or a chat." Never "Get started", "Unlock", "Supercharge", "🚀".
- **Own failures lightly.** "Oops! Looks like the feed is unavailable right now. Come back
  later or contact me." / "Oops! Looks like something is updating, will be back soon."
- **Name sources the way a reader would.** "The article feed is unavailable", not
  "contentful is unavailable".
- **No filler headings.** A gallery needs no "Gallery" heading on screen; the name goes to
  the screen reader.

## Mechanics

- Sentence case for titles, buttons and labels. Uppercase comes from CSS on labels, so the
  source text stays "Experience", not "EXPERIENCE".
- Em dash with spaces between a role and its company, a status and its title
  ("404 — Not found"), or in a page title ("WebGl terrain mesh — h3nr7").
- Middot with spaces between a name and title or a run of items
  ("Henry YP Ho · Platform & Product Engineer", "React · Three.js · AWS").
- Counts in parentheses qualify an instruction: "Show more (12)".
- Accessible names say where a link goes: "Book a meeting — opens the booking page",
  "Open the profile site"; platform names alone in a social row ("GitHub", not "Henry on GitHub").
- No exclamation marks except the "Oops!" that opens an apology.
